# Financing pro forma

Start with the entity, consolidation scope, reporting date, currency and unit scale. Match the proposed funding to that opening basis. If closing occurs later, identify intervening balance movements before presenting a closing position.

## Build the bridge

1. Obtain opening cash, current assets and liabilities, total assets, liabilities and equity. Separate current and noncurrent borrowings from lease liabilities. Preserve the source and locator for each amount.
2. Classify each transaction independently. Split new borrowings and existing debt repayments by current and noncurrent portion. Separate equity contributions, capital expenditure and amounts expensed and paid at closing.
3. Confirm the accounting and cash assumptions. This model capitalizes the full capital-spending amount. The cash expense reduces cash and equity immediately. Separately identify items such as deferred financing fees, restricted cash or tax effects that require a different bridge.
4. Use the [input contract](references/contract.md) and the [standalone example](README.md) to calculate each alternative from the same opening balances. Never turn an unknown inflow or use into zero.

## Review the result

Reconcile closing cash to opening cash plus new funding less repayments, capital spending and closing expenses. Check assets against liabilities plus equity. Check the change in net debt separately against capital spending plus the cash expense less the equity contribution.

Report the use of proceeds with the resulting cash and debt. State whether the figures include leases and which cash balance offsets gross debt. When a case has a funding gap, report its size and identify the unresolved funding source. The model leaves closing metrics open in that case.

Debt-to-equity uses positive closing equity. A current ratio requires positive current liabilities. A null ratio means the denominator falls outside that definition. Assess earnings, future debt service and covenant headroom using separate forecasts and the applicable terms.
